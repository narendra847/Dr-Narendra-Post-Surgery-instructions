Oesophageal Outpouch Surgery

Zenker's Diverticulum Repair

Cricopharyngeal Myotomy

What Was Done

A Zenker's diverticulum is a pouch (outpouching) that forms in the wall of your food pipe (oesophagus) at the back of your throat. This pouch can trap food and cause swallowing difficulties.

During your surgery, I made an incision in the left side of your neck to access the area. The tight muscle at the top of your food pipe (cricopharyngeal muscle) was carefully divided to relieve the pressure that caused the pouch to form. The pouch itself was removed using a surgical stapler.

The wound was closed in layers and sealed with medical skin glue.

VERY IMPORTANT: Your Diet Plan

You MUST follow this diet progression carefully to allow your food pipe to heal properly.

You will have a swallow test the morning after surgery

If the test is satisfactory, you can start the diet plan below

4-Week Diet Progression

1 Weeks 1-2: Free Fluids Only

You can drink ANY liquids that flow freely

You CAN have:

  • • Water, tea, coffee
  • • Milk, milkshakes, smoothies
  • • Fruit juice (no pulp)
  • • Clear soups and broths
  • • Nutritional supplement drinks (e.g., Ensure, Sustagen)
  • • Ice cream, jelly, custard
  • • Protein shakes

You CANNOT have:

  • • Any solid food
  • • Thick smoothies with chunks
  • • Yoghurt with fruit pieces
  • • Anything you need to chew

Tip: Use a straw if it helps you drink more comfortably. Aim for at least 2 litres of fluid per day.

2 Weeks 3-4: Pureed Foods

Food must be smooth like baby food - NO lumps or chunks

You CAN have:

  • • Smooth pureed soups
  • • Mashed potato (very smooth)
  • • Pureed vegetables
  • • Pureed meat or fish (blended with gravy or sauce)
  • • Smooth yoghurt (no fruit pieces)
  • • Scrambled eggs (very soft and moist)
  • • Pureed fruit (like apple sauce)
  • • Smooth porridge or cream of wheat
  • • All fluids from weeks 1-2

You CANNOT have:

  • • Any food with lumps or chunks
  • • Bread (even soft bread)
  • • Rice or pasta
  • • Raw vegetables or fruits
  • • Nuts or seeds
  • • Anything that requires chewing

Tip: Use a blender or food processor. Food should be smooth enough to pass through a straw (though you don't have to drink it through a straw).

3 After Week 4: Return to Normal Diet

After 4 weeks, you can gradually return to your normal diet. Start with soft foods and slowly progress to regular foods.

  • Introduce one new food at a time
  • Chew food thoroughly
  • Eat slowly and take small bites
  • If any food causes difficulty, go back to softer foods for a few more days

Wound Care

You CAN

  • Shower normally - your wound is sealed with medical glue
  • Let water run over the wound
  • Pat dry gently with a clean towel
  • The glue will fall off by itself in 1-2 weeks

You CANNOT

  • Take baths for 4-6 weeks
  • Go swimming for 4-6 weeks
  • Use hot tubs or spas for 4-6 weeks
  • Pick at or remove the glue - let it fall off naturally

Pain Management

Neck surgery can cause discomfort, especially when swallowing. This is normal and should improve over the first week.

Regular Pain Relief

  • Paracetamol: Take 2 tablets every 6 hours
  • Ibuprofen: Take 400mg every 8 hours with food (or liquid nutrition)
  • Continue for up to 1 week or until pain settles

Stronger Pain Medicine (If Needed)

Use prescription pain medicine (opiates) only if pain is severe and regular pain relief is not enough.

Activity and Recovery

  • Mobilise: Get up and walk around as soon as you feel able after surgery
  • Walk regularly to prevent complications
  • Gradually increase your activity each day
  • Rest when you feel tired
  • Avoid strenuous activity or heavy lifting for 2 weeks
  • Return to work when comfortable (usually 1-2 weeks depending on your job)

When Can I Drive Again?

Follow these rules before getting back behind the wheel:

1. Wait at least 1 week

No driving for the first week after surgery

2. Test yourself first

Make sure you can comfortably wear a seatbelt and make an emergency stop without pain

3. No driving on opiates

Do not drive if you are taking prescription pain medicine (opiates)

4. Check with your insurance

Contact your insurance company to confirm you're covered to drive after surgery

Warning Signs

Contact your doctor immediately if you experience:

  • Increasing difficulty swallowing or complete inability to swallow
  • Severe neck swelling or swelling that gets worse
  • Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  • Change in voice that gets worse or doesn't improve
  • Increasing redness, swelling, or pain at the wound site
  • Fever (temperature above 38°C)
  • Discharge or fluid from the wound
  • Coughing up blood

Follow-Up Appointment

You need to see me again in 4-6 weeks.

Please call my private rooms to make this appointment.
